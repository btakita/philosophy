# Permaculture

## [Ontology](./ontology.md)

Any system of sustainable agriculture that renews natural resources and enriches local ecosystems.

The design, installation and maintenance of indefinitely sustainable human communities set in balanced ecologies, both urban and rural.

### Universal Applicability Analysis

Per the Universal Applicability Principle, every ontology term applies to permaculture **at every scope** — not as an exclusive label, but as a universal descriptor.

| Term | How it applies to permaculture |
|------|-------------------------------|
| [Existence](./existence.md) | Permaculture is a design discipline *within* Existence; it treats the land, the humans, and the non-human life as co-members of the same whole. Nothing designed by permaculture is outside the system it inhabits. |
| [Entity](./entity.md) | Every element of a design (a tree, a swale, a chicken, a household) is an Entity. Permaculture names and places Entities rather than commanding them. |
| [System](./system.md) | A permaculture site is a [System](./system.md) of Systems: each guild, bed, and watershed is itself a whole of relationships. The designer sees Systems at every [resolution](./resolution.md). |
| [Domain](./domain.md) | "Permaculture" names a bounded [domain](./domain.md) of design knowledge, but each site is also a domain with its own [language](./language.md) (soil type, microclimate, local species, household needs). |
| [Scope](./scope.md) | Scale-of-intervention is explicit — zone 0 (home) through zone 5 (wild) is literally a [scope](./scope.md) hierarchy. The zones map bounded sub-scopes, while "the land" as a whole is effectively unbounded at the design horizon. |
| [Context](./context.md) | "Design from patterns to details" means: establish [context](./context.md) first (climate, water flow, legal boundaries, human needs), then place elements. Context is never skipped. |
| [Resolution](./resolution.md) | The same site is modeled at multiple [resolutions](./resolution.md) simultaneously — a bioregion zoomed out, a garden bed zoomed in. Moving between resolutions is the core design act. |
| [Focus](./focus.md) | Finite human attention (hours of labor, years of observation) is treated as a conserved resource. "Obtain a yield" and "use small and slow solutions" are [focus](./focus.md)-preservation principles. |
| [Perspective](./perspective.md) | "Observe and interact" privileges the land's [perspective](./perspective.md) over the designer's preconception. A guild is designed *from the plants' side*, not from the gardener's abstract plan. |
| [Abstraction](./abstraction.md) | Zones, sectors, and guilds are [abstractions](./abstraction.md) — simplified models that make a complex site designable. They are useful because the designer knows the map is not the territory. |
| [Pattern](./pattern.md) | The whole discipline is [pattern](./pattern.md)-first: spirals, branching, edges, stacking functions. Details are derived from patterns, never the reverse. |
| [Consciousness](./consciousness.md) | Permaculture assumes gradient [consciousness](./consciousness.md) across Entities — soil microbes, plants, animals, humans — and designs for mutual signal exchange rather than unilateral control. |
| [Evolution](./evolution.md) | A design is never finished; it is expected to [evolve](./evolution.md) as conditions change. "Creatively use and respond to change" is Holmgren's twelfth principle. |
| [Story](./story.md) | Every permaculture site carries a [story](./story.md) — the history of its soil, water, people, and interventions — and the designer inherits and extends that story rather than starting from zero. |
| [Precision](./precision.md) | A permaculture plan mixes [precise](./precision.md) local detail (this swale, this depth, this species) with intentionally imprecise universal principles (catch and store energy). Both are necessary; neither suffices alone. |

**Bounded vs. unbounded scope in permaculture:**

- **Bounded** — any individual site, season, guild, or water budget. These scopes have explicit limits and invite precise design.
- **Unbounded** — the ethical triad (earth care, people care, fair share) and the twelve principles. These operate at an unbounded [scope](./scope.md) because they must remain true across every site, climate, and culture. Their imprecision is a feature: it makes them transferable.

The discipline's strength comes from refusing to collapse these two modes. Principles without sites become slogans; sites without principles become random interventions.
