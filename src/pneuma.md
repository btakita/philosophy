# Pneuma

## [Ontology](./ontology.md)

Pneuma (πνεῦμα) is the animating [energy](./energy.md) of an [entity](./entity.md) — the "breath" that makes a [system](./system.md) a living whole rather than an inert aggregate. Across traditions, pneuma names the [signal](./signal.md) that carries [life](./life.md), [spirit](./spirit.md), and [consciousness](./consciousness.md) through matter.

Related terms: [spirit](./spirit.md), [energy](./energy.md), [consciousness](./consciousness.md), [life](./life.md), [being](./being.md).

## [Axiology](./axiology.md)

Pneuma is what distinguishes a living [system](./system.md) from its static description. A [body](./body.md) without pneuma is a corpse; a [conceptual system](./conceptual-system.md) without pneuma is a dead diagram. Pneuma is the moving [perspective](./perspective.md) that keeps an [entity](./entity.md) [evolving](./evolution.md) with its [context](./context.md).

In [Stoic](./stoicism.md) physics, pneuma is the active principle (ποιοῦν) that pervades passive matter and gives it structure, tension (*tonos*), and qualities. In Christian usage, πνεῦμα ἅγιον is the Holy Spirit — the indwelling presence that [communicates](./communication.md) between the divine and the human. In medical Greek, pneuma is literal breath — the [signal](./signal.md) that reveals whether an organism is still alive.

Pneuma is observable by its effects, not by direct inspection. You see the [pattern](./pattern.md) of movement it induces, not the thing itself.

## [Epistemology](./epistemology.md)

### [Cultural](./culture.md) Definition

<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pneuma" target="_blank">pneuma (wiktionary)</a>

> ### Noun

> (philosophy, theology) The vital spirit; the soul.

> (historical, medicine) The air breathed; breath.

<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneuma" target="_blank">Pneuma (Wikipedia)</a>

> Pneuma (πνεῦμα) is an ancient Greek word for "breath", and in a religious context for "spirit". It has various technical meanings for medical writers and philosophers of classical antiquity, particularly in regard to physiology, and is also used in Greek translations of ruach רוח in the Hebrew Bible, and in the Greek New Testament.

<a href="https://www.etymonline.com/word/pneuma" target="_blank">pneuma (etymonline)</a>

> "the vital spirit, soul," from Greek pneuma "a blowing, a wind, blast; breeze; influence; breathed air, breath; odor, scent; spirit of a person; inspiration, a spirit, ghost," from pnein "to blow, to breathe".

### [Pattern](./pattern.md) Expression

Pneuma manifests wherever an [entity](./entity.md) exhibits self-sustaining motion that cannot be reduced to its parts:

- **Biology** — respiration, the breath cycle that keeps cells metabolizing
- **Software** — a running process, the "live" state of a system vs. its source code at rest
- **Community** — the shared [energy](./energy.md) that animates a [collective](./collective.md) beyond its member list
- **Conversation** — the flow that keeps a dialogue moving, as distinct from the transcript

In each case, pneuma is the [signal](./signal.md) that the [system](./system.md) is currently a [perspective](./perspective.md) and not merely a record of one.
